Image-generation brief · companion to RK-MERCH-01

Merch prompt kit

Copy-ready prompts for generating Raktify merchandise visuals — product photography, fabric, drape, light and placement. Hand this to whoever runs the image model. Read the band below first; it is the difference between usable output and output that has to be thrown away.

Leave the wordmark zone blank — the logo is composited afterwards

Image models cannot render the Raktify wordmark. They will produce a confident lookalike in an invented typeface, with the droplet in the wrong place and the ™ missing or mangled — which is exactly the drift the vector exists to prevent. Every prompt here asks the model for an empty placeholder area instead.

The real wordmark-tm.svg is then placed into that area in Figma, Illustrator or Photoshop. The model does fabric, folds, light and composition — what it is genuinely good at. It never does the logo.

Master prompt

Start here

One prompt that establishes the whole visual world: the fabric, the light, the restraint. Run this first, judge the look, then use the variants to change the garment without losing the world.

Master — quiet-luxury product still

4:5 · photographic 
Editorial product photograph of a single heavyweight cotton garment, shot for a quiet, expensive clothing label rather than for an event.

GARMENT: an oversized boxy short-sleeve t-shirt in warm undyed cream (#fdf8f4), 260 gsm garment-dyed ring-spun cotton with a faintly uneven, lived-in surface. Tubular body, no side seams. Ribbed collar, twin-needle hem. It is styled flat-lay on a warm sand-coloured paper backdrop (#f5ece4), one sleeve folded naturally across the body so the fabric holds soft, honest creases — not pressed flat, not artificially crumpled.

BRANDING AREA: on the wearer's left chest, leave a completely EMPTY rectangular area approximately 50 mm wide by 16 mm tall, positioned 75 mm below the shoulder seam and 60 mm in from the centre front. This area must be clean, unbranded blank fabric — absolutely no text, no letters, no logo, no symbol, no mark of any kind. A brand mark will be added later in post-production. The fabric weave and lighting must be even and undistorted across this whole area.

LIGHT: single large soft north-facing window light from the upper left, no flash, no reflector. Gentle directional falloff so the weave and the fold shadows read clearly. Warm neutral white balance. Deep but soft shadows in the folds; no blown highlights.

MOOD: restrained, tactile, expensive, calm. The kind of garment photographed for a Japanese or Scandinavian slow-fashion brand — the fabric is the subject. Nothing is shouting. No styling props, no accessories, no flowers, no plants, no coffee cups, no scattered objects.

COLOUR: strictly a warm palette — cream, sand, undyed natural cotton, warm near-black, and one deep earthen red (#b8231a) reserved for the brand mark only, which is NOT in this image. No blues, no greens, no purples, no cool greys, no teal.

CAMERA: 85 mm prime look, shot slightly above and square on, shallow but not blurry depth of field, fine natural film grain. Photorealistic, high resolution, no illustration, no 3D render look.

ASPECT: 4:5 vertical.

Why it reads as luxury: the fabric is the subject, the light is single-source and soft, and there is no styling clutter. Everything the model is asked to render is a material property. The brand is absent by design — which is what leaves room for it to be quiet.

Variants

Swap one thing at a time

Six parallel options, not a sequence — run whichever the deliverable needs. Each one changes the garment, surface or framing while keeping the master’s light, palette and restraint. Keep the blank-placeholder paragraph in all of them.

Composite checklist

Before anything is shared

The model output is a background plate. These six steps turn it into an approved Raktify visual, and this is a sequence — each step depends on the one before it.

  • 01Reject any plate with invented lettering. If the model has put text, a mark or a symbol into the placeholder area, discard it and re-run. Do not paint it out — the fabric under it is now wrong.
  • 02Place the real vector. Import frontend/public/wordmark-tm.svg. Scale it by height and let the width follow at 3.135 : 1. Never scale the axes independently, never retype, never recolour.
  • 03Bend the mark into the cloth. Use a subtle displacement or warp driven by the plate’s own fold shadows, and blend at 88–95% so the weave shows through. A mark sitting perfectly flat on a creased garment is the tell that reads as fake.
  • 04Check the ™. It ships inside the vector, already sized and positioned. Do not enlarge it, restyle it, or move it — and never substitute ®. The trade mark application is pending.
  • 05Verify the palette. Sample the composite: cream #fdf8f4, sand #f5ece4, warm ink #1c1917, brand red #b8231a, droplet #dc2f1d. Any blue, green or cool grey that crept in during grading comes out.
  • 06Label it as a visual, not a proof. These images are for pitch decks, social posts and internal review. They are not production artwork — the vendor works from RK-MERCH-01, and only a physical sample approves a run.

Two treatments still need founder sign-off

The droplet used on its own, without its red icon square, is technically a new icon variant — and it is also the strongest quiet-branding device available here (variant E rests on it). Tonal-on-tonal embroidery collapses the wordmark’s two-colour split, since Rakt red and ify warm ink become one thread colour. Both are deliberate design-system exceptions, not oversights. Neither goes to a vendor until it is signed off.
